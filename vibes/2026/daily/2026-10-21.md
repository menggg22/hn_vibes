## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The inference layer is the main battlefield right now — DeepInfer's dominance today reflects a broader pattern where the interesting open problem has shifted from "which model is smartest" to "who can serve it cheapest and fastest." The SlopCodeBench entry naming specific models and "xhigh considered harmful" suggests the community is developing real critical vocabulary for evaluating inference quality versus cost tradeoffs.
- [DeepInfer: High-performance inference framework for DeepSeek models](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50163277) — 2099pts — worth reading for the architectural choices: what does "high-performance" mean specifically here, and how does it differ from vLLM/SGLang?
- [SlopCodeBench 10/2026 – Opus 5.5, Astra wins overall, xhigh considered harmful](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50108717) — 142pts — "xhigh considered harmful" is the real story; what failure mode did they find at extreme sampling temperatures that earns a Dijkstra reference?
- [Google open-sources TorchTPU, a native PyTorch back end for TPUs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50169053) — 242pts — open-sourcing the TPU backend is a strategic move worth understanding; Google closing the gap between their hardware and the dominant framework.

**agent-systems**: The anxiety around agents is maturing from "will they work?" to "what do they do when I'm not watching?" — the Dead Man's Switch, the cost-capping Ask HN, and the AI code ownership thread all cluster around the same underlying problem: agents are running unsupervised and the accountability infrastructure doesn't exist yet. RuneScape Bench using MMOs as an agent evaluation environment is a genuinely novel methodological signal.
- [RuneScape Bench: MMOs for Agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50136126) — 240pts — MMOs as agent evals is worth exploring: they offer long-horizon tasks, sparse rewards, social interaction, and economic systems that pure coding benchmarks completely lack.
- [How abliterated models can get you pwned](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50159245) — 225pts — the security implications of jailbroken/abliterated models running in agent pipelines deserves careful reading as the DHS DeepSeek ban and CA compromise land on the same day.

**Deep dives**:
- [EMudhra Intermediate CA Compromise](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50166579) — the CA trust model is the load-bearing wall of HTTPS; understanding exactly how this compromise happened and what the revocation response looks like is essential reading on a day when 8 more TLS certs are also flagged.
- [Publish fake stats, get cited in LLMs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50165801) — a closed loop of misinformation that didn't exist two years ago: fabricate a statistic, get it into training data, watch it cited authoritatively forever; the mechanism is worth understanding in detail.
- [WhatsApp's help pages hid LLM instructions: don't recommend a regulator](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50159341) — 711pts — the specific instruction revealed ("don't recommend a regulator") is the detail that makes this consequential rather than just sneaky; what other behavioral instructions are embedded in consumer AI deployments?
