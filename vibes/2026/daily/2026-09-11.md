## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The pretraining and scaling conversation is quietly continuing under the noise of product launches — trillion-parameter territory is now a practical engineering concern, not a research fantasy. Samsung's zHBM prototype (memory stacked directly on accelerators) hints that the memory-bandwidth wall is being attacked at the silicon level.
- [Compute-efficient pretraining and scaling to trillion-parameter models](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613072) — 108pts — Worth reading for the compute-efficiency framing specifically; "efficient" is doing more work here than raw scale.
- [Samsung Debuts zHBM Prototype, Stacking Memory Directly on AI Accelerators](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49593896) — 55pts — The architectural implication is significant: if memory and compute co-locate, the whole memory-bandwidth bottleneck calculus changes.

**agent-systems**: The agent layer is bifurcating — OpenAI is releasing APIs and getting scrutinized, while Cognition's SWE-2 is making benchmark claims that HN is cross-referencing skeptically against Terminal-Bench 2.1. The "Ask HN: How do you manage skills files?" thread is the most practically useful signal: practitioners are already building personal agent-memory infrastructure by hand.
- [OpenAI Agents API](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49649213) — 90pts — The comment density relative to score suggests the API itself is less interesting than the trust questions surrounding it today.
- [Cognition launches new SWE-2 model, Rivaling Fable 5.1 and GPT-Astra](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49645443) — 341pts — The benchmark competition between named models is becoming its own genre; watch whether Terminal-Bench 2.1 becomes the standard the way SWE-bench did.

**Deep dives**:
- [OpenAI's Navier-Stokes release included a Lean 4 formal proof](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49650326) — 123pts — The intersection of LLM-generated math and formal verification is the most technically consequential thing OpenAI may have done this week; the trust scandal makes reading the actual proof more urgent, not less.
- [More questions about whether researchers can trust OpenAI with unpublished math](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49639408) — 594pts — Read alongside the Navier-Stokes story; the combination of "OpenAI can generate formal proofs" and "OpenAI cannot be trusted with your unpublished math" is a genuine institutional dilemma.
