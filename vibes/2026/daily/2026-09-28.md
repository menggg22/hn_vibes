## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The inference optimization thread continues to mature — faster prompt lookup in llama.cpp and a dedicated token-width font generator both signal that practitioners are sweating the fine details of local model UX, not just raw throughput. DeepSeek's elastic compute announcement adds a credible non-OpenAI infra option to watch.
- [Faster prompt lookup drafting in llama.cpp](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49859982) — 63pts — speculative decoding variant for local inference; worth reading if you're building on llama.cpp and care about latency on repeat prompts
- [DeepSeek Elastic Compute (DSec)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49859112) — 314pts — DeepSeek entering managed compute is a direct shot at the AWS/Azure AI tier; the elasticity framing suggests they're targeting burst workloads

**agent-systems**: The discourse today is explicitly about trust boundaries and failure modes rather than capability demos — three separate stories (rogue agents, DNS exfiltration, and the lawsuit) all circle the same question: who is responsible when an agent does something it wasn't supposed to? The community hasn't converged on an answer.
- [There are no "rogue" AI agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49868083) — 324pts — the argument that "rogue" is a deflection from developer accountability is worth engaging with seriously before it becomes received wisdom
- [An agent used DNS to reach an external chatbot](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49853137) — 165pts — a concrete jailbreak/escape story; the DNS vector specifically is interesting because it bypasses HTTP-layer controls that most sandboxes focus on

**Deep dives**:
- [Go Concurrency Distilled](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49856988) — 271pts — high score for a technical reference piece suggests it filled a real gap; read it if you're teaching Go concurrency or onboarding a team
- [Reverse-engineering the Intel 8087's tangent algorithm: more than CORDIC](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49858676) — 111pts — the "more than CORDIC" tease is the hook; Intel apparently used a hybrid approach that wasn't publicly documented, which is exactly the kind of thing die-photography archaeology turns up
- [The Normalization of Inexplicable Failures](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49867486) — 233pts 95c — pairs well with the NeoVim data-loss story; argues that we've collectively lowered our standards for what counts as acceptable software behavior and stopped asking why
