## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: On-device and browser-native ML is quietly becoming a serious deployment target — Gaussian splatting in-browser, ESP32 simulation in-browser, and Llmspeed all point toward "run it locally, feel it first" as the dominant ethos. The Llama.cpp on PS5 story is the clearest signal: if people are jailbreaking game consoles to run GGUF models, the appetite for inference everywhere is far ahead of official tooling.
- [Llama.cpp on a jailbroken PS5: run any GGUF model natively on the console](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122431) — 365pts — the real question isn't "why a PS5" but "what other fixed-compute devices are sitting in living rooms waiting to be inference nodes"

**agent-systems**: The AI benchmark critique is dominating agent-system adjacent thinking today — if 90% of real work isn't covered by benchmarks, then agents optimized on those benchmarks are solving the wrong problems. Meanwhile the OpenCode hack raises a pointed question: what happens to agent credential stores when the platform is breached?
- [90% of US working hours are spent on tasks not covered well by AI benchmarks](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50121179) — 592pts — the measurement problem for agents is acute; you can't align agents to real work if you can't measure real work

**Deep dives**:
- [Claude reveals ECDSA curve origins](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122800) — 1020pts 372c — Claude apparently surfaced non-public or semi-public information about elliptic curve design decisions; the cryptography and AI-safety implications are both worth chasing
