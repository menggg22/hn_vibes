## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The on-device story is maturing fast — Xiaomi MiMo v2.6, Kev (Qwen3.5-based decision models), and Mini-AGI (8GB VRAM) all landing the same day suggests the "runs locally on your hardware" benchmark is becoming table stakes for model launches. The interesting tension is whether continual learning (Mini-AGI's claim) can survive contact with reality or whether it's still catastrophic-forgetting theater.
- [Frontier AI on Your Own Hardware](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49791647) — 83pts — worth reading alongside the Xiaomi and Kev drops to understand what "frontier" actually means at the edge in late 2026

**agent-systems**: CI is emerging as the unexpected chokepoint in agentic coding workflows — when agents write code faster than humans review it, the pipeline backs up at the gates humans still control. The Foremerge and Viaduct Show HNs both attack coordination problems between parallel agents, which is the right problem six months ahead of mainstream adoption.
- [AI coding has made CI a bottleneck, so we reworked ours to keep up](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49792067) — 115pts — practitioner-level writeup on a problem that's about to become every engineering org's problem; the architectural decisions here will be cargo-culted widely

**Deep dives**:
- [Exfiltrate your Weights](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49771110) — 720pts, still climbing — the highest-scored story today by raw points; model weight exfiltration as an attack surface is underexplored and this framing will seed a wave of security research
- [What Sun got wrong](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49787436) — 482pts — historical autopsies of Sun Microsystems always generate great HN discussion because the parallels to current platform companies are uncomfortably obvious; read the comments
- [Attention is all you have](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49787726) — 552pts — the title riff on the transformer paper suggests a darker thesis about cognitive resource depletion in an always-on AI world; 158 comments suggests HN found it resonant enough to argue about
