## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: Inference optimization is quietly maturing on alternative hardware — AMD is no longer a punchline in the vLLM context, and the Engrim SQLite memory engine suggests the community is building the persistence and tooling layer that makes local-first ML actually usable day-to-day rather than a demo.
- [Speculative Decoding in vLLM on AMD GPUs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49596054) — 135pts — worth reading for the concrete throughput numbers on non-NVIDIA silicon; the gap is closing faster than most assume

**agent-systems**: The simultaneous downtime of all major AI APIs and the flurry of sandboxed-environment tooling reveals the same anxiety: agents running in production have no graceful degradation story and no persistent memory by default, and builders are patching both problems from the bottom up.
- [Coop – Isolated VM Environments for Running Claude Code and Codex](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49593842) — 56pts — isolation-first approach to running coding agents; the framing acknowledges agents are not trustworthy enough to run unwrapped, which is honest and useful

**Deep dives**:
- [Trusting-Trust Attack against an Entire Linux Distribution](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49575515) — Thompson's classic attack re-implemented at distro scale; essential reading this week given the CA key factoring story running alongside it
- [I've factored the RSA keys of a Certificate Authority from the 90s](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49604637) — 172pts — a working factorization of a legacy CA's keys is not just historical curiosity; it's a live reminder of what "deprecated" actually means in a world of long-lived signed artifacts
- [The Dataflow Model Revisited](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49589190) — 93pts — the original Millwheel/Beam paper was foundational; a revisit in 2026 with the benefit of a decade of production streaming systems is worth the time for anyone designing event-driven architectures
