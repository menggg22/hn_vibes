## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The Platonic Representation Hypothesis getting formalized proof via JEPAs is quietly one of the most important theoretical results in a while — it suggests models trained differently converge to the same internal geometry, which has massive implications for model merging, transfer, and efficiency. Meanwhile Memdoor's multi-agent ensemble beating Fable 5 on LiveCodeBench Hard shows small open models in coordination are catching frontier coding benchmarks.
- [Proving and improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50325454) — 105pts — if representations truly converge, you can swap encoders freely; read this before the next wave of model-merging papers cites it
- [Memdoor: A team of 5 Qwen3.8-27B models beats Fable 5 on LiveCodeBench Hard](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50320995) — 423pts — the cost/performance crossover point for open vs. closed frontier is arriving faster than expected

**agent-systems**: Two data points pulling in opposite directions: Memdoor shows coordinated small agents genuinely outperforming flagship models on hard coding tasks, while the Minecraft civilization experiment confirms agents collapse into coordination overhead at scale. The gap between narrow-task swarms and general-purpose agent societies remains enormous.
- [AI agent swarms in Minecraft fail to build civilization, drown in bureaucracy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50325662) — 148pts — a controlled environment with clear goals and they still failed; the bureaucracy metaphor will stick in agent architecture discussions for months

**Deep dives**:
- [TCP Use-After-Free: CVE-2026-98432 / CVSS 9.8](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50319143) — CVSS 9.8 in the TCP stack itself; read the technical thread before your sysadmin asks you about it tomorrow
- [It's the memory latency, stupid](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50318720) — 253pts, systematic argument that memory latency is the actual bottleneck everyone keeps ignoring while optimizing compute; directly relevant to LLM inference optimization
- [AI, Lean, and the Future of Formal Verification, with Lean Creator Leo de Moura](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50290652) — pairs perfectly with "Mathematics has changed" above; de Moura explaining how AI and Lean interact is the primary source for that debate
