## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The conversation has decisively shifted from "can we run it" to "at what cost per token and on whose hardware" — the Kimi K3 on MI355X post represents a maturing infrastructure discourse where AMD vs NVIDIA economics matter as much as model quality. On-device is quietly advancing through oddities like an autoregressive LM on a 6502 processor, which is either art or a proof of concept for the ages.
- [Running Kimi K3 on MI355X at Better Performance per Dollar Than B300](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49141073) — 202pts — AMD's MI355X beating B300 on price-performance is the kind of competitive data point that reshapes infrastructure decisions; worth reading the methodology carefully

**agent-systems**: Agent tooling is fragmenting into micro-projects rather than consolidating — Mu, MicroCodex, Nanocodex, and Authoryze all shipped within 24h, none breaking out, suggesting the space is either pre-paradigm or oversaturated. The real agent-adjacent action is in the Ask HN thread questioning why agents need "skills" at all, which cuts to an architectural disagreement nobody has resolved.
- [Show HN: MicroCodex Coding Agent – OpenAI/codex reimplemented in C++ <1MB binary](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49147842) — 12pts — the sub-1MB constraint is the interesting design decision; worth asking what you lose and whether it matters for most use cases

**Deep dives**:
- [F*: A general-purpose proof-oriented programming language](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49143925) — 146pts 64c — proof-oriented languages are having a quiet moment as formal verification interest grows; the comment ratio suggests people are actually engaging with the technical substance
