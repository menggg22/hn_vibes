## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: On-device and consumer-hardware inference is pushing into increasingly aggressive territory — a 125B MoE on two 16 GB GPUs and Llama.cpp on a jailbroken PS5 both dropped today, signaling that the "you need a data center" assumption is eroding faster than the cloud providers would like. Apple's MLX ecosystem is also maturing enough to anchor a serious research release.

- [Llama.cpp on a jailbroken PS5: run any GGUF model natively on the console](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122431) — 143pts — the PS5 has 16 GB of unified memory and a capable GPU; if the jailbreak community standardizes this, it's a meaningful new inference surface worth watching for edge deployment patterns
- [Apple ML Research: Muse Spark Open with MLX on Mac Studio](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118320) — 252pts — Apple open-sourcing a model optimized for MLX signals they want third-party developers building on the stack, not just using it; the Mac Studio angle means serious on-device creative AI workloads are now a first-class target
- [Designing more efficient quantum circuits with Claude](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50121939) — 82pts — LLM-assisted circuit synthesis is a narrow but high-value domain; the interesting question is whether Claude is pattern-matching known optimizations or generalizing in ways that surprise the researchers

**agent-systems**: The agent layer is fragmenting into specializations — orchestration (Termexo, rStation), observability (Weatherdeck), and wait-time UX (the CS 1.6 match) are all being built independently, suggesting the "single agent does everything" phase is over and the integration/tooling phase has begun. The Claude Code review post landing at 189 comments also suggests practitioners are hungry for real process detail, not just capability demos.

- [How the Claude Code team does code review](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122951) — 201pts — rare insider look at how a team that builds an AI coding tool actually uses it internally; the gap between their described workflow and most readers' experience is probably generating most of the 189 comments
- [Show HN: rStation – A shared research lab for your AI agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118876) — 49pts — multi-agent shared context is the unsolved coordination problem; worth watching whether they've found a useful primitive or just a pretty dashboard

**Deep dives**:
- [90% of US working hours are spent on tasks not covered well by AI benchmarks](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50121179) — read this today because it's the most direct challenge to the "benchmarks = capability" assumption and the methodology will determine whether the 322-comment argument is actually settled
- [Big Fish: Exploiting a 37-Year-Old Kernel Bug in OpenBSD](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118977) — classic long-tail kernel archaeology; the OpenBSD team's reputation for security makes a 37-year-old exploitable bug especially instructive about what "secure by default" actually means in practice
- [Postgres 19 delayed after AI reviewers find more bugs than humans can fix](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50119991) — this is the most interesting second-order AI effect of the week: AI QA is now fast enough to outpace human patch throughput, which raises the question of whether AI should be writing the fixes too or whether that collapses the review loop entirely
