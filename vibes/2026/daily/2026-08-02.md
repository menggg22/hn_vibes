## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The push to do serious ML on constrained hardware is accelerating — 8GB GPU post-training experiments and Linux on ESP32 signal that the community is actively working against the "you need datacenter scale" narrative. AMD's MI450 getting a dedicated kernel optimization guide suggests the GPU monoculture around NVIDIA is cracking at the optimization layer.

- [Attention Decode on AMD MI450 GPUs: A Gluon Kernel Optimization Guide](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49077646) — 67pts — worth reading for anyone betting on AMD as a serious inference platform; the gap between theoretical FLOPS and real attention throughput is where the battle is being fought

**agent-systems**: The "agentic loop" framing is giving way to something more structured — today's Ask HN "When did we go from agentic loops to graphs?" captures a real architectural shift happening in production systems. The `qm` multiplayer agent harness landing at 648pts suggests the community is converging on the idea that agents need coordination primitives, not just better prompts.

- [qm – Multiplayer agent harness for work](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49126604) — 648pts — the "multiplayer" framing for agents is the most interesting thing here; it implies agents need turn-taking and shared state, not just parallelism

**Deep dives**:
- [The Art of 64-bit Assembly](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49134599) — 172pts 84c — with AI generating most boilerplate code, understanding what's happening at the metal is becoming a differentiating skill again; read this alongside the register deprivation piece
- [RipGrep musl binaries occasionally segfault during very-large searches](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49133889) — 241pts 162c — a worked example of musl vs glibc behavioral divergence under memory pressure; essential reading for anyone shipping statically linked binaries in production
- [Is AI reasoning right for the wrong reasons?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49124358) — 199pts 229c — the comment-to-score ratio is a tell; this is generating genuine unease about whether reasoning benchmarks measure what we think they measure, and the answer appears to be "not reliably"
