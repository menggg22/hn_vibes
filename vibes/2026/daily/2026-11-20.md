## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The "run it locally, run it small" movement is maturing from hobby to competitive product category — this week brings sub-20MB browser inference, 2-bit coding models, and a WebGPU weight-space demo, all shipped independently, suggesting the toolchain has quietly crossed a usability threshold. The question is no longer *can* you run a model on-device but *which tradeoffs* (size, accuracy, latency) are acceptable for which tasks.
- [Pete Warden on local AI, open models, voice interfaces, the future of hardware](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50561508) — 212pts — Warden has been right about on-device before; his current take on voice + open models as the wedge is worth parsing carefully given today's tiny-model Show HNs

**agent-systems**: The agentic coding tool space hit 975 catalogued entries (per ADE Index), which is both a milestone and a warning sign — the space is fragmenting faster than any single team can track, and the Ask HN about why backlogs aren't empty despite AI suggests agents are still failing at the messy last mile of real codebases. The FBI/Claude story adds a new dimension: agents operating on sensitive conversations now have legal surface area.
- [Show HN: ADE Index – a sourced and ranked index of 975 agentic coding tools](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50554973) — 25pts — 975 tools is a market-structure data point as much as a directory; someone should build the Wirecutter for this space

**Deep dives**:
- [Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50563148) — 367pts — not "AI helps physicist" but apparently autonomous derivation of novel results; the comments will be arguing about what "solve" means and that argument is itself the read
