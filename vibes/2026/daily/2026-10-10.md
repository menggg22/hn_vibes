## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: The headline story is aggressive compression — Whistle at 16.9 MB for speech-to-text signals that the "make it tiny" race is accelerating faster than the "make it smarter" race for on-device use cases. DeepSeek 4.1 Flash fits the same pattern at the model level.
- [Whistle: Speech to Text in 16.9 MB](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50008427) — 915pts — the number is the argument; worth reading to understand what architectural choices get you there and whether quality held

**agent-systems**: Two stories today converge on the same latent problem: agents are hard to trust because they're hard to watch. The screen-annotation tool is a UX answer; "Why are coding agents so dumb?" is the frustration that motivates the question.
- [Show HN: Let your AI agents paint big arrows, boxes and text on your screen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50018817) — 364pts — worth studying as a design pattern: externalizing agent intent as spatial annotation rather than log output
- [Why are coding agents so dumb?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50020947) — 70pts — good framing exercise; the comment section likely contains sharper diagnoses than the post itself

**Deep dives**:
- [ADHD as a circadian rhythm disorder: evidence and implications for chronotherapy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50011928) — 382pts 253c — reframes a widely-diagnosed condition through a biological clock lens; the chronotherapy angle has immediate practical implications and the comment section will be full of people testing it against their own experience
- [OpenAI, the Partition Principle, and Mathematics](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50013902) — 195pts 295c — high comment-to-score ratio suggests this is the thread where people are actually working through the epistemics of AI math claims, not just reacting
