## 🔭 Depth — ml-infra / on-device ML / agent-systems

*What's the trend and what sparks curiosity.*

**ml-infra & on-device ML**: Consumer hardware is eating what was server-only workloads six months ago — 125B parameter models at 100 tokens/second on a single RTX 4090 is a genuine inflection point, and the Rust metadata emission speedup story shows the toolchain layer catching up to model growth. Apple Intelligence's disk bloat being a topic at all suggests on-device ML is now a storage and resource management problem, not just a capability problem.
- [Run Qwen 3.8 Flash Next (125B) on consumer hardware (RTX 4090) at 100T/s](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49953495) — 568pts — worth digging into the quantization and batching techniques that made this possible; the gap between "possible" and "practical" is closing fast
- [Emitting metadata early makes building/checking Rust up to twice as fast](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49951218) — 123pts — compiler-level optimization that halves iteration time for large Rust ML projects; directly affects anyone building inference engines in Rust

**agent-systems**: The "agents need documentation not memory" post is the most intellectually interesting agent-systems framing in weeks — it reframes the agent reliability problem as a knowledge representation problem rather than a context window problem. This is the kind of architectural opinion that tends to age well.
- [Agents don't need memory, they need documentation](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49945933) — 342pts — challenges the dominant "longer context = smarter agent" assumption; worth reading before designing any agent retrieval architecture

**Deep dives**:
- [Improper redaction reveals Google Data Center water and electricity usage](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49957068) — rare empirical data on hyperscaler resource consumption that Google clearly didn't want public; directly relevant to AI infrastructure cost discussions happening everywhere today
- [Treachery in the Rodin Museum 3D scan verdict](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49946355) — the legal and ethical framework around who owns a 3D scan of a public-domain physical object is unsettled law with massive implications for digital archives and AI training data
- [Homa: The end of TCP for AI clusters](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49957117) — with AI clusters now the dominant buyer of datacenter networking, a credible TCP replacement claim deserves scrutiny; worth watching the video before the paper makes the rounds
